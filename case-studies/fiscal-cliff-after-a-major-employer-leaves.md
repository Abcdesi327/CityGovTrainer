## Background

Riverbend is a city of about 38,000 operating under a council-manager charter. A
single manufacturer has anchored the local economy for four decades, employing roughly
1,400 people directly. The general fund runs about $42 million. Reserves stand at
roughly three months of operating expenditures against an adopted policy floor of two
months. The council has seven members; three are in their first term, and two face
reelection in fourteen months.

## The situation

In March, the manufacturer's parent company announced it will consolidate operations
out of state, closing the Riverbend facility at the end of the fiscal year. Finance
staff estimate the direct and indirect loss at roughly 9% of general fund revenue,
phasing in over two years as payroll, utility, and sales tax effects work through. The
property tax effect lands later still, on the reassessment cycle.

## The decision point

The manager must transmit a proposed budget in eight weeks. The proposal has to take a
position on how much of the gap to absorb with reserves versus recurring reductions.

## Complicating factors

- Three councilmembers have publicly committed to no service reductions and no layoffs.
- The finance director's five-year forecast shows the gap persisting past year three,
  meaning it is structural rather than one-time.
- The city's bond rating agency has a scheduled review nine months out.
- Two department heads have privately signaled they will retire rather than manage cuts.
- A regional economic development authority is pitching a redevelopment plan for the
  site with a five-to-ten year horizon — real, but not a near-term revenue answer.

## Materials

Build out as teaching props: a one-page five-year forecast table; a draft transmittal
memo; a service-level reduction menu with dollar values and FTE impacts.

## Discussion questions

1. Which parts of this gap are one-time and which are structural? What evidence
   distinguishes them?
2. How should the manager handle the three councilmembers' public commitment — engage
   it directly in the transmittal memo, or leave it to the council to reconcile?
3. What does the manager owe the bond rating agency, and when?
4. At what point, if any, does the redevelopment plan belong in the revenue forecast?
